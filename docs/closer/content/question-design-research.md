# CLOSER – question-design research

**Updated:** 18 August 2026
**Purpose:** Research-informed editorial guidance for CLOSER questions
**Scope:** All shipped packs and routes, including OFF SCRIPT and YOUTH WORKSHOP (14–17)

This document is a design synthesis, not a claim that the new CLOSER packs or individual prompts have been scientifically validated. The bilingual [question catalog](question-catalog.de-en.md) remains the editorial source of truth for shipped wording and routes. The focused FR-018–020 evidence programme, candidate banks, and cross-pack audit are indexed separately in [research](../research/README.md).

---

## 1. Executive summary

Research does not provide a ready-made validated question list for every CLOSER mode. It does, however, identify mechanisms that can guide safer and more effective editorial choices:

1. **A question alone does not create closeness.** Reciprocal self-disclosure, responsive listening, and feeling understood matter together.
2. **Emotional meaning is usually more useful than fact collection.** “Why did that matter to you?” often contributes more than another biographical detail.
3. **Intensity should rise gradually.** Highly intimate disclosure too early can create discomfort or suspicion when little trust exists.
4. **Positive is not the same as shallow.** Specific gratitude, good news, shared memories, shared laughter, and future plans can all support connection.
5. **Depth is not synonymous with trauma or maximum vulnerability.** Identity, values, pride, belonging, hope, and being understood can be equally deep.
6. **Short routes need their own arc.** Evidence supports short, time-bounded closeness exercises, but not random excerpts from a longer pack.
7. **Late Night requires distinct safety rules.** High-quality sexual communication may support satisfaction, but prompts must never turn disclosure into pressure or implied consent.
8. **Context-specific packs need context-specific limits.** Road safety, family autonomy, and workplace power cannot be handled by generic consent copy alone.

**Classic is immutable.** Its questions, translations, order, and route membership must not change as part of work on newer packs. Its proximity to the original 36-question structure has independent product value. Research should primarily shape the newer packs, route arcs, safety constraints, and occasional listening cues.

## 2. What the literature supports

### 2.1 Reciprocal, progressively deeper self-disclosure

The original Fast Friends procedure combines reciprocal self-disclosure with a gradual increase in personal intensity. Its first study reported greater perceived closeness than a small-talk condition. It does **not** show that 36 questions reliably cause people to fall in love, or that every altered list will reproduce the result. [Aron et al. (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003)

A meta-analysis found three robust relationships: people who disclose more intimately tend to be liked more; people disclose more to those they already like; and people often like others more after disclosing to them. Context and degree moderate these effects. [Collins & Miller (1994)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7809308/)

Disclosure that is too intimate for the relationship stage can create suspicion rather than trust. First Date and other low-familiarity modes therefore need a gentle ramp. [Rubin (1975)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103175800254)

Implications for CLOSER:

- Both people should usually answer the same core prompt.
- Starting order should alternate visibly and fairly.
- Act I should stay light and concrete, Act II become more personal, and Act III become more meaningful.
- A route may end without forcing the maximum available level of intimacy.

### 2.2 Feeling understood is part of the mechanism

Process models describe intimacy as self-disclosure combined with perceived partner responsiveness. Emotional disclosure predicted intimacy more strongly than factual disclosure. [Laurenceau, Barrett & Pietromonaco (1998)](https://www.affective-science.org/pubs/1998/LaurenFBPl1998.pdf)

A 42-day diary study with married couples also found that self-disclosure, the other person’s disclosure, and perceived responsiveness predicted daily intimacy. [Laurenceau et al. (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15982109/)

People who ask more questions, particularly genuine follow-up questions, tend to be liked more. Perceived responsiveness mediated the effect, which also appeared in speed-dating data. [Huang et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28447835/)

Recent conversation studies associated connection in deep conversations with verbal validation and follow-up questions; a brief intervention increased these listening behaviors. [West et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)

CLOSER may therefore use a few brief, non-instructional response cues:

- “Ask for one specific example.”
- “What about that mattered most to the person?”
- “In one sentence, say what you understood.”
- “No solution is needed—listen first.”

Use them sparingly so the experience does not feel clinical or didactic.

### 2.3 Turn-taking is better than two consecutive interviews

Experimental studies reported more liking, closeness, and enjoyment when people alternated answers to personal questions rather than having one person answer a complete set before switching. Perceived responsiveness and feeling liked appear to explain part of this effect. [Sprecher et al. (2013)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002210311300070X), [Sprecher & Treger (2015)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/pere.12090)

Default behavior should therefore alternate question by question. Interviewing one person for a whole block can remain an intentional twist, not the baseline structure.

### 2.4 People underestimate deeper conversations

People often expect deeper conversations to be more awkward and less caring than they ultimately experience them. Experiments found deeper conversations produced more connection and enjoyment than participants predicted. [Kardas, Kumar & Epley (2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34591541/)

CLOSER can invite genuine depth without equating depth with heaviness. Copy such as “A deep answer may also be joyful, hopeful, or light” can reduce unnecessary apprehension.

## 3. Route duration

The 45-minute original is not the only researched form of structured closeness conversation:

- The Relationship Closeness Induction Task used 29 progressively personal prompts in about nine minutes. Many prompts are student-specific and are not directly suitable for modern CLOSER packs. [Sedikides et al. (1999)](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~crsi/The%20Relationship99.pdf)
- Other studies retained progressive intimacy in three timed stages lasting approximately 12–24 minutes in total. One recent protocol used three eight-minute blocks and explicitly allowed people to skip questions. [Sprecher et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407512459033), [24-minute protocol (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12909909/)
- A video-based protocol used three five-minute blocks. [15-minute protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7901865/)

Use **curated routes with duration ranges**, not arbitrary question counts:

| Route | Editorial target | Arc | Typical use |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Quick | 8–20 min, pack-dependent | self-contained three-act miniature | first dates, breaks, events, low-commitment entry |
| Standard | 20–45 min, pack-dependent | three recognizable intensity stages | default for most packs |
| Full | 40–90 min, pack-dependent | complete arc with room for follow-ups | Classic, Date Night, Couples, Deep |

These are pilot ranges, not promises. The catalog contains the current pack-specific values. Quick questions must be curated rather than randomly sampled. Good follow-ups may mean fewer prompts are completed. Passing must always remain available and must not be framed as failure. Only unmodified Classic Full should be described as closely following the original research protocol; new packs and short routes are research-informed.

## 4. Editorial direction by pack

The examples below illustrate mechanisms. They are not individually validated and are not a substitute for the wording in the question catalog.

### 4.1 Classic

**Goal:** Preserve the established 36-question experience with minimal editorial interference.

- Retain the current questions and order.
- Do not mix in suggestive, humorous, or pack-specific replacement prompts.
- Use only occasional, clearly optional listening cues.
- Describe Classic precisely as based on a research protocol designed to generate interpersonal closeness, not as proof that people will fall in love.

### 4.2 First Date

**Goal:** Curiosity, comfortable self-disclosure, and conversational continuity without premature boundary crossing.

Prefer small joys and pride, interests with personal meaning, comfort and everyday life, hopeful future images, and prompts that naturally invite a follow-up. Avoid early prompts about trauma, death, family conflict, sex, former relationships, or a presumed life model.

Example patterns:

- “What has put you in an unexpectedly good mood lately, and why?”
- “Tell me about a small recent moment you felt proud of.”
- “What quality in someone new helps you feel comfortable quickly?”
- “What does an ordinary but genuinely good day look like to you?”

### 4.3 Date Night

**Goal:** Warmth, attraction, playful novelty, and renewed attention.

Shared novel or stimulating activities may improve experienced relationship quality and counter boredom. Prompts should not only analyze the past; they can prepare small new shared experiences. [Aron et al. (2000)](https://files.blogs.baruch.cuny.edu/wp-content/blogs.dir/7309/files/2019/11/Aron_Norman_Aron_McKenna_Heyman_2000.pdf)

Prefer specific moments of closeness or attraction, playful wishes without obligation, appreciative observations, small shared adventures, and later-act sensual questions that remain non-explicit. For example:

- “What recent moment between us felt especially close?”
- “What side of me do you still enjoy rediscovering?”
- “What small thing could we try together for the first time?”
- “What kind of compliment genuinely reaches you?”

### 4.4 Couples

**Goal:** Care for an existing relationship, make positive experiences visible, and understand needs without simulating couples therapy.

Sharing positive events is particularly relationship-supportive when the other person responds actively and constructively. [Gable et al. (2004)](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Gable-Reis-Impett-and-Asher-2004.pdf)

Daily gratitude predicted greater connection and satisfaction the following day. “Other-praising gratitude”—naming the other person’s quality or intention, not only one’s own benefit—appears especially useful. [Algoe, Gable & Maisel (2010)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01273.x), [Algoe, Kurtz & Hilaire (2016)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1948550616651681)

Prefer specific gratitude, reactions to good news, support preferences, small recurring signs of connection, and shared novelty. Ask what response is wanted during stress instead of assuming advice is helpful; active listening during stress disclosure is associated with better relationship experiences. [Bodie et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29863374/)

### 4.5 Friends

**Goal:** Appreciation, mutual understanding, useful support, and a shared future.

Prefer specific shared memories, admired qualities, support preferences, unspoken changes, and new plans. Keep the framing explicitly platonic and avoid treating friendship as a lesser form of romance.

### 4.6 Old Friends

**Goal:** Reactivate shared history while learning who the person is now.

Nostalgic memories frequently involve close people and meaningful events and can strengthen social connectedness. [Wildschut et al. (2006)](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~crsi/WildschutSedikidesArndtRoutledge2006.pdf)

Specific, one-time shared “we-memories” are more useful than broad nostalgia; activating and sharing them has been causally linked to greater closeness. [Beike, Cole & Merrick (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28395590/) Sharing autobiographical memories also produced more closeness than sharing impersonal information in a cross-cultural study. [Guan & Wang (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1177%2F00220221211072809)

Prefer a concrete shared scene, a forgotten detail, how an old event changed meaning, what remained stable, and who the person can be now. Do not presume reconciliation, restored intimacy, or a specific future.

### 4.7 Deep

**Goal:** Emotional meaning, identity, and feeling understood without becoming a collection of maximally distressing topics.

Prefer less-visible but not necessarily secret aspects of identity, hopes and values, times someone felt understood, emotional expression, and the personal meaning of belonging, courage, success, or safety. As an editorial balance, no more than roughly one third of prompts should primarily activate difficult material. Hope, pride, connection, and future orientation are also forms of depth.

### 4.8 Chaos

**Goal:** Shared laughter, surprise, playful novelty, and co-creation—not exposure or humiliation.

A meta-analysis distinguishes relationship-supportive humor from aggressive or demeaning humor. Shared affiliative laughter and playful co-creation are the appropriate direction for CLOSER. [Hall (2017)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pere.12183) Shared laughter may also act as a behavioral indicator of relationship well-being. [Kurtz & Algoe (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1111/pere.12095)

Prefer inventing something absurd together, unusual small joys, playful alternate realities, shared rituals, and surprising but non-shaming disclosure. Avoid humiliation, forced imitation, body judgment, required secrets, public tasks, and adversarial “which of you is worse” prompts.

### 4.9 Late Night (18+)

**Goal:** Explicit adult communication about desire, boundaries, preferences, and safety, with active opt-in and no pressure to act.

A meta-analysis of 93 studies covering 38,499 people found substantial associations between sexual communication and both relationship and sexual satisfaction. Communication **quality** mattered more than frequency. This supports good conversation practices, not pressure for maximum explicitness. [Mallory (2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34968095/)

Reciprocal sexual self-disclosure may contribute to satisfaction through better mutual understanding of preferences and dislikes. [MacNeil & Byers (2005)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0265407505050942)

Consent is multidimensional: internal willingness and externally communicated signals are not the same. The absence of “no” is not a reliable “yes.” [Muehlenhard et al. (2016)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00224499.2016.1146651), [Jozkowski et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24452630/)

Appropriate prompt types ask how someone wants to be asked, how they recognize their own yes/maybe/no, what makes an honest no or pause easier, which preferences or fantasies they only want to discuss, and what aftercare or space feels good.

Required guardrails:

- An explicit 18+ notice and one shared introduction asking both people to check directly with each other before starting.
- Clear initial and finale copy that answers never constitute consent to an action; avoid repeating UI rituals that displace attention from the conversation.
- Consent is specific, contextual, voluntary, and withdrawable at any time.
- Passing and ending remain available without tokens or explanations.
- No surprise unlock from a non-explicit pack.
- No `NO THINKING`, countdown, `PREDICT`, proxy answers, or forced eye contact.
- No assumptions about gender, orientation, monogamy, relationship status, bodies, or experience.
- No task that the app expects or asks people to confirm they completed.
- Discovery may be discreet, but hiding or showing the pack is separate from the shared per-session participation check.

### 4.10 Road Trip

**Goal:** Use travel time for a playful, progressively more meaningful conversation about the current journey, travel memories, preferences, and future possibilities.

Travel is a useful editorial frame for concrete memories and shared novelty, but it creates a non-negotiable safety boundary. Passenger conversation is itself a possible distraction. Simulator research suggests that an in-car passenger may adapt a conversation to changing traffic demands better than a remote phone partner, but that study did not test a structured conversation game and does not establish that ROAD TRIP is safe while driving. [Drews, Pasupathi & Strayer (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19102621/)

The NHTSA advises people to make the passenger the designated phone user or to park before using a phone, and Austrian official guidance prohibits drivers from reading or operating phones outside narrow permitted uses. [NHTSA distracted-driving guidance](https://www.nhtsa.gov/campaign/distracted-driving), [oesterreich.gv.at: Handyverbot am Steuer](https://eausweise.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/mobilitaet/kfz/10/4/Seite.063220)

Required design constraints:

- The initial release permits play only in a parked vehicle or when neither participant is driving. A future passenger-led moving-vehicle design requires a separate safety decision and validation.
- Quick is the only route that may be offered while a participant is driving, and it must still not be described as safe, certified, or distraction-free. Parking remains the preferred condition.
- Standard and Full require a parked vehicle or a setting in which neither participant is responsible for driving.
- Environmental attention always overrides an answer. A stopped or absent answer is not a failure or reason for feedback.
- Disable `NO THINKING`, countdowns, `PREDICT`, simultaneous-answer twists, timers, eye-contact prompts, device handoffs, auto-advance, surprise audio, and any task involving the surroundings, navigation, photography, or music selection.
- Use no private moment. End Quick directly; make any Standard or Full reflection visibly optional.
- Do not presume cars, international travel, unrestricted mobility, a passport, a particular budget, or a romantic relationship.

Pilot ranges are Quick 15–20 minutes, Standard 30–45 minutes, and Full 50–70 minutes. They are not safety claims or completion targets. Local traffic rules and the real road situation always take precedence.

### 4.11 Family

**Goal:** Help two adults who understand each other as family see one another beyond fixed roles, exchange useful knowledge about care and boundaries, and imagine a freely chosen future.

Family narrative studies linked coordinated storytelling, perspective-taking, and validation with measures of family satisfaction, functioning, and self-esteem. These studies used conventional family samples, often including children or adolescents, and the relevant findings are mainly correlational; they do not validate an adult two-person question game. [Koenig Kellas (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637750500322453), [Bohanek et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1545-5300.2006.00079.x)

The pack must explicitly include chosen family. A study of 350 young adult gay and bisexual men found chosen family in many participants’ own family definitions, but that specific sample cannot define family for everyone. Participants’ own language remains authoritative. [Soler et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13178-017-0288-7)

Required design constraints:

- Include family of origin, adoptive and blended family, relatives through partnership, and chosen family without assuming biology, a shared home or childhood, harmony, current closeness, parenthood, or frequent contact.
- Let different versions of a shared memory stand beside one another. The game must not search for a single correct family story.
- Move from concrete everyday stories to roles and support, then to appreciation, autonomy, boundaries, and a future that need not be closer.
- Do not presume reconciliation. Qualitative work on adult sibling estrangement found meaningful variation in whether reconciliation was wanted. [Blake, Bland & Rouncefield-Swales (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0192513X211064876)
- Never ask people to rank relatives, assign blame, diagnose someone, disclose another person’s secret, justify estrangement, or mediate an active third-party conflict.
- Use no private moment at launch. A secret appreciation task can create pressure even when positive. Disable `PREDICT`, and do not use `NO THINKING` for roles, support, autonomy, or boundaries.
- Describe the pack as conversation, not therapy, mediation, reconciliation, healing, or an assessment of family functioning.

Pilot ranges are Quick 15–25 minutes, Standard 30–50 minutes, and Full 55–85 minutes. All three routes need their own positive-to-personal arc, unconditional passing, and a direct or clearly optional finale.

### 4.12 Colleagues

**Goal:** Help two voluntary peers understand practical working preferences without turning the conversation into an assessment, conflict process, or substitute for organizational responsibility.

Team research associated psychological safety with learning behavior, and research on managerial openness linked authority behavior, perceived safety, and employee voice. These findings concern organizational conditions and power; they do not show that a conversation game creates psychological safety or better performance. [Edmondson (1999)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999), [Detert & Burris (2007)](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.26279183)

Employees may withhold work concerns because speaking can carry interpersonal or career consequences. Friendly copy cannot remove that risk. [Milliken, Morrison & Hewlin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-6486.00387)

Required design constraints:

- Position the pack for people on as equal a footing as possible. Exclude manager–direct-report, evaluator–evaluated, client–dependent-contractor, and other pairings in which one person materially affects the other’s pay, schedule, tasks, promotion, discipline, contract, or access to opportunities.
- Participation must be optional in substance. Do not use the pack for mandatory team building, onboarding, performance review, training attendance, mediation, investigation, grievance handling, or HR assessment.
- Ask about everyday work rhythm, communication, coordination, capacity signals, feedback preferences, strengths, recognition, learning, and sustainable boundaries—not loyalty, personality ranking, active conflict, or judgments of colleagues and employers.
- Do not solicit confidential work information, named complaints, salary, health, disability, union activity, family status, religion, politics, romantic or sexual information, or other sensitive personal data. General, anonymized, hypothetical, or passed answers must remain valid.
- Do not store, score, transcribe, export, or report answers. Nothing said is a promise, assignment, admission, consent to a work change, or valid basis for an employment decision.
- Launch Quick first. Release Standard only after moderated peer testing; keep the remaining 12 master questions as editorial reserve. Do not create a public Full route merely to match other packs.
- Use no private moment, `PREDICT`, `NO THINKING`, simultaneous-answer twist, countdown, or performance-oriented timer. Alternate starters and keep any listening cue sparse and transparent.

Pilot ranges are Quick 15–25 minutes and Standard 30–50 minutes. If a serious safety, discrimination, harassment, legal, or employment concern emerges, the game ends; it must not imply that a prompt can investigate or resolve the issue.

### 4.13 Off Script

OFF SCRIPT translates shared novelty and co-creation into lightweight activities rather than deeper disclosure. The evidence supports shared novel activity as a plausible relationship mechanism, not these particular cards. The pack therefore stays cooperative and unscored, makes no closeness claim, and uses a short pilot length to avoid performance fatigue.

Every card must work without sensors or captured environmental data and include a low-attention interpretation: seated, small, quiet, or hands-only. The participant—not the app—decides whether an object or setting is safe and available. Passing is the correct behavior when it is not.

### 4.14 Youth Workshop (14–17)

This first release deliberately selects one age band rather than presenting the same disclosure task to children and adults. UNESCO's safe-learning guidance emphasizes inclusive environments free from bullying, humiliation, exclusion, and unequal power. That supports light peer prompts, no social-knowledge tests, no embarrassment mechanics, and a facilitator boundary; it does not validate this question bank. [UNESCO (2026)](https://www.unesco.org/en/health-education/safe-learning-environments)

The privacy baseline follows data-minimization and high-privacy-by-default principles: the pack asks for no age proof, records no answers, recommends nicknames or initials, and creates no resumable game. The UK ICO Children's Code is used as conservative design guidance rather than a claim about the exact Austrian legal classification of CLOSER. GDPR duties and Austrian/EU applicability still require qualified review if data processing or institutional deployment changes. [GDPR Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [ICO Children's Code](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/the-children-s-code-and-children-s-online-privacy/)

The route excludes topics that can expose protected, evaluative, clinical, family, safeguarding, or confidential information in a shared-device workshop. A real concern must go to the institution's established safeguarding channel; a game must never become a disclosure or reporting substitute. Broad release requires educator, safeguarding, accessibility, bilingual, and moderated review with the target age group.

## 5. Editorial schema and review matrix

Each question should have stable editorial metadata in addition to its text:

```text
id
pack
act
intensity
estimatedSeconds
answerMode              # both | alternating | one_then_other
responseCue             # none | follow_up | validate | reflect | celebrate
valence                  # positive | neutral | mixed | difficult
temporalFrame            # past | present | future | hypothetical
sensitiveTopics[]
twistAllowed
lateNightSafeTwists[]
quickRouteEligible
routeMembership[]       # quick | standard | full | editorial_reserve
contextEligibility      # pack-specific runtime conditions
privateMoment           # none | explicitly reviewed pack-specific design
safetyConstraints[]
```

Score each candidate from 0–2 on every criterion before inclusion:

| Criterion | Review question |
|---|---|
| Pack fit | Would this prompt clearly belong to the pack without seeing its title? |
| Reciprocity | Can both people answer it meaningfully? |
| Openness | Does it allow more than yes/no or one “correct” response? |
| Specificity | Does it invite a scene, example, or observable experience? |
| Meaning | Does it make room for why something matters or how it felt? |
| Intensity | Does it fit the act and likely starting closeness? |
| Responsiveness | Does it invite a natural, respectful follow-up? |
| Inclusivity | Does it avoid assumptions about gender, sexuality, family, health, and lifestyle? |
| Safety | Can someone pass without being made responsible for the other person’s feelings? |
| Duration fit | Can it realistically be answered within the intended route? |

Reject a prompt if it requires diagnosis, judgment, or ranking of other people; assumes trauma, partnership, parenthood, monogamy, or sexual experience; confuses disclosure with a dare; asks someone to predict another person’s boundaries or consent; combines several large prompts; or is only interesting with an extreme answer.

## 6. Supporting mechanics

### Sparse response cards

After selected questions, CLOSER may show one brief cue:

- **FOLLOW UP:** “Ask about one detail you do not yet understand.”
- **REFLECT:** “What was the most important point in that answer?”
- **CELEBRATE:** “Take a moment to celebrate with them before sharing your own story.”
- **VALIDATE:** “You do not need to solve it. First, show that you heard them.”

### Positive-first arc

Couples and Deep should not begin with deficits. A robust sequence is:

1. a specific positive experience;
2. personal meaning;
3. a wish or need;
4. possible vulnerability;
5. hope or a shared future.

### Soft endings

At the natural end of a short route, the app may offer a low-pressure end or continuation. This respects different conversation speeds and rewards the conversation rather than list completion.

Milestone animation should celebrate shared time, not grade disclosure depth, speed, or consent. It belongs at act transitions and natural completion, should not obstruct navigation, and must have a meaningful reduced-motion treatment.

## 7. Limits and validation

Marketing, onboarding, and engineering documentation must keep these limitations explicit:

- Short-term laboratory closeness is not the same as a permanently improved relationship.
- Many studies use Western, student, or heterosexual samples; transfer to all ages, cultures, queer relationships, and neurodivergent people cannot be assumed.
- Correlations with relationship satisfaction do not prove that a specific CLOSER prompt causes that outcome.
- Only the original protocol may be closely associated with its study result. The other packs are new editorial products.
- ROAD TRIP has not been validated as safe while a vehicle is moving. Passenger control and a shorter route reduce device interaction; they do not eliminate distraction.
- FAMILY is not family therapy, mediation, reconciliation, or evidence of healthy family functioning.
- COLLEAGUES cannot create organizational psychological safety or neutralize power, retaliation, confidentiality, or employment risk.
- OFF SCRIPT activities are editorial prototypes; shared-novelty findings do not validate a particular task or prove a social benefit.
- YOUTH WORKSHOP is limited to peer pairings aged 14–17 and is not a safeguarding, assessment, participation, or conflict-resolution tool.
- “Research-informed” or “based on findings about self-disclosure and active listening” is appropriate. “Scientifically proven” or “validated” is not.

Before broad release, test every pack with varied pairs. Moderated sessions should rate situation fit, answerability, conversation quality, intensity, inclusive wording, passing frequency, and willingness to play again. Include varied ages, relationship types, sexual orientations, cultural backgrounds, and neurodivergent participants. Late Night additionally requires a dedicated safety, consent, and legal review. Road Trip needs parked and public-transport pilots first; any passenger-led moving-vehicle study requires a dedicated safety protocol and still cannot certify the game as safe. Family needs varied biological, adoptive, blended, infrequent-contact, and chosen-family pairs. Colleagues needs voluntary peer sessions across sectors and work arrangements, with Standard evaluated separately from Quick.

## 8. Editorial priorities

### P0 – before broad release

1. Preserve Classic wording and make its research claim precise.
2. Maintain an explicit intensity curve for every pack.
3. Enforce Late Night guardrails technically and editorially.
4. Curate Quick routes rather than sampling them randomly.
5. Keep all person and role language gender-neutral and assumption-free.
6. Keep the implemented Road Trip release parked/no-participating-driver only unless a separately validated safety design replaces that restriction.
7. Keep Family non-prescriptive and Colleagues voluntary, peer-oriented, confidential, and free of assessment mechanics.
8. Keep OFF SCRIPT cooperative, sensor-free, adaptable, and free of public or risky performance.
9. Keep YOUTH WORKSHOP non-resumable, peer-only, non-evaluative, and within the approved topic exclusions.

### P1 – highest quality gain

1. Use a small number of response cards for follow-up, reflection, celebration, and validation.
2. Center Couples on gratitude, positive events, support preferences, and shared novelty.
3. Center Old Friends on specific we-memories rather than general nostalgia.
4. Balance Deep across vulnerability, hope, identity, and positive meaning.
5. Keep Chaos focused on co-creation and shared laughter rather than exposure.
6. Center Road Trip on travel-specific memories and preferences, Family on multiple perspectives and autonomy, and Colleagues on practical working preferences rather than personal evaluation.

### P2 – after initial user testing

1. Score questions with the review matrix.
2. Calibrate duration ranges from real sessions.
3. Replace prompts with unusually high pass rates or low conversation quality.
4. Iteratively evaluate and refine pack-specific Quick, Standard, and Full routes.
5. Do not promote Colleagues reserve questions into a Full route without a separate product decision and safety review.
6. Validate the owner-approved conversation-only **POWER, BY CHOICE** implementation through the named specialist, bilingual, accessibility, privacy/legal, physical-device, and moderated-session gates. Do not infer validation from implementation.
7. Validate the owner-approved SLOW BURN sequence through the memo's specialist, ethics, accessibility, privacy/legal, adverse-event, physical-device, and staged-session gates. The current consumer boundary permits explicitly named touch at the chest or breasts, buttocks, and inner thighs, while excluding genitals, anus, penetration, breath or neck play, restraint, impact, and surprise touch.
8. Validate the implemented FR-020 set—37 original atomic changes plus three full-bank addenda—through bilingual paraphrase interviews, contextual specialist review, and moderated sessions. Keep Classic's 36 questions unchanged and preserve catalog/runtime/test synchronization for every later edit.
9. Validate OFF SCRIPT across seated, quiet, public, low-mobility, and limited-space settings before adding a longer route.
10. Validate YOUTH WORKSHOP with safeguarding, education, accessibility, bilingual, privacy, and moderated age-group review before broad institutional promotion.
