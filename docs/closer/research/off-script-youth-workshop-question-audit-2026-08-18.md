# CLOSER question audit — OFF SCRIPT and YOUTH WORKSHOP

**Audit date:** 18 August 2026  
**Scope:** all 48 bilingual master cards, both Quick/Standard routes, act framing, pack positioning, privacy copy, direct finales, persistence behavior, catalog/runtime fidelity, and the YOUTH WORKSHOP facilitator guide  
**Change status:** all 19 bilingual rewrites, two route changes, framing, finales, privacy copy, and facilitator guidance were owner-approved and implemented atomically on 18 August 2026  
**Release decision:** OFF SCRIPT is suitable for moderated usability testing with the implemented accessibility patch. YOUTH WORKSHOP is **not ready for broad institutional promotion** until safeguarding, privacy, educator, bilingual, accessibility, and target-age reviews are complete.

> **Decision boundary.** `Retain` means that this desk review found no specific reason to replace the card now. It is not scientific validation. `Rewrite` identifies copy changed through the owner's atomic implementation decision. Evidence about novelty, participation, accessibility, privacy, or adolescent disclosure does not validate any CLOSER card or route.

## 1. Executive decision

The two banks are structurally sound: 24 stable bilingual IDs each, three eight-card acts, nine-card Quick and eighteen-card Standard routes, six reserves, unconditional Pass, no timer, no twists, no Private Moment, no required contact, and no exact duplicate copy elsewhere in the catalog. Runtime and catalog membership are synchronized.

The full audit finds:

| Disposition | OFF SCRIPT | YOUTH WORKSHOP | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Retain current card wording | 12 | 17 | 29 |
| Rewrite card wording | 12 | 7 | 19 |
| Route-only change | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Unique cards with an implemented intervention | 13 | 8 | **21** |
| Cards audited | 24 | 24 | **48** |

The two route-only IDs are outside the rewrite set: OFF SCRIPT Q24 enters Standard, while YOUTH WORKSHOP Q15 leaves both playable routes. The intervention union is therefore 19 rewritten IDs plus two route-only IDs: 21 unique cards.

The highest-priority findings are:

1. **YOUTH WORKSHOP must not promise peer confidentiality.** The app can truthfully say that answers are not typed in or recorded by CLOSER. It cannot guarantee that a partner will not remember or repeat spoken content. The positioning, direct finale, and facilitator script currently overstate that boundary.
2. **Voluntary participation must be operational, not merely stated.** A neutral alternative, no explanation for passing, no reward for completion/disclosure, no answer collection, and a known safeguarding route remain mandatory. This is consistent with UNICEF's current adolescent-participation checklist and Austrian school safeguarding guidance.
3. **YOUTH WORKSHOP Q15 has left both routes.** A personal-strength prompt can become informal assessment or role assignment in a facilitated setting. The more general, accessibility-supportive Q14 is now routed after its wording change.
4. **OFF SCRIPT needs a real multimodal contract.** “Seated,” “quiet,” and “hands only” do not cover people who cannot see the referenced detail, use the required gesture, vocalize, hear a sound, synchronize movement, or answer under a fixed time cue.
5. **OFF SCRIPT should remove unnecessary performance control and timing.** Q14 makes one participant the director of the other; Q12 and Q21 add exact seconds although timing is not essential. All three can keep the playful construct without those constraints.
6. **Neither pack may be described as validated or beneficial.** Shared-novelty work was conducted largely with romantic couples and does not validate OFF SCRIPT. Participation and safeguarding guidance supports design constraints, not an outcome claim for YOUTH WORKSHOP.

## 2. Audit standard and evidence transfer

Every card was reviewed for construct, presupposition, voluntariness, power, disclosure exposure, accessibility, bilingual equivalence, route position, repetition, finale interaction, and evidence overreach.

The strongest applicable sources establish constraints rather than card efficacy:

- UNICEF's [Adolescent participation checklist](https://www.unicef.org/adolescentkit/reports/tool-adolescent-participation-checklist) requires participation to be voluntary, accessible, respectful, understandable, meaningful, and safe. It specifically warns that participation often privileges the most confident adolescents. This supports the neutral alternative and multimodal design; it does not validate paired disclosure.
- UNICEF's [adolescent safeguarding guidance](https://www.unicef.org/adolescentkit/reports/tool-responding-child-protection-concerns) requires a known reporting route and trained, sensitive handling of real concerns. CLOSER must not become intake, investigation, or a substitute for that route.
- Austria's current [school safeguarding circular and template](https://rundschreiben.bmbwf.gv.at/media/2024_31_kinderschutzkonzept.pdf) places external providers and safeguarding procedures within the school's protection system. A product guide cannot replace the institution's own duties or process.
- The UN Committee on the Rights of the Child's [General Comment No. 25](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=ktgN5mdZ3%2F7HI71TICFndxUbgjCMzv5eAsQlExjVrUTq1qVeuKFH5zRonEHQJ%2F5RDd48K%2FyDdI8qZaaGERQHHg%3D%3D) says children's digital participation should not create undue monitoring or data collection that violates privacy, thought, or opinion.
- GDPR Articles 5 and 25 require purpose limitation, data minimisation, and data protection by design and default in applicable processing ([official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679)). Austria's Data Protection Authority also stresses clear, simple, child-directed information and explains the national age threshold for consent to information-society services ([DSB guidance](https://dsb.gv.at/ueber-die-datenschutzbehoerde/teens-kids)). These sources do not determine the lawful basis or controller roles for a specific school deployment.
- The UK ICO [Children's Code standards](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/code-standards/) are conservative product guidance, not a claim that UK law governs this product. They support minimal collection, high-privacy defaults, separate choices, and no privacy-weakening nudges.
- W3C WCAG 2.2 requires digital instructions not to rely solely on sensory characteristics and provides enough-time principles ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). CAST UDL 3.0 recommends choice, varied response and movement methods, and multiple media for communication ([CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/)). Applying those principles to a two-person activity is an editorial accessibility inference, not a WCAG conformance claim for spoken play.
- A qualitative school-group study found that disclosure among classmates could support connection but could also interact with bullying and harassment; voluntary participation, group composition, and treatment of shared information were important ([Kvist Lindholm & Zetterqvist Nelson, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/1754730X.2015.1044253)). It studied a mental-health intervention with schoolgirls, not light CLOSER prompts, so transfer is limited to the social-risk warning.
- Recent mixed-method adolescent work identifies privacy concerns as a barrier to disclosure ([Kullberg et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jad.70100)). Its small Dutch interview sample and distress-topic focus do not justify encouraging disclosure here; they strengthen the case for honest confidentiality limits.
- Shared novel activity has been associated with relationship-quality outcomes in romantic couples ([Aron et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.273)). That population, relationship context, and experimental task do not validate OFF SCRIPT, adolescents, friends, or these exact activities.

### OFF SCRIPT route and mechanic decision

- **Quick 9:** membership is retained after the wording changes. It still needs physical sessions because its Act II selection is performance-heavy.
- **Standard 18:** replace Q22 with Q24. The route then ends with a shared title/motto instead of an access-fragile spoken museum label; Q22 becomes Reserve after its rewrite.

- **Direct finale:** replace the comparative `which idea deserves applause?` prompt. Applause can create a winner, require sound, and turn completion into performance.
- **No new mechanic:** do not add scoring, voting, sensors, camera/microphone capture, public reveal, performance reward, or a Full route before sessions support it.

### YOUTH WORKSHOP route and mechanic decision

- **Icebreaker 9:** replace Q15 with rewritten Q14. Proposed membership becomes Q01, Q02, Q04 / Q09, Q12, Q14 / Q17, Q20, Q22.
- **Workshop 18:** replace Q15 with rewritten Q14. Q15 becomes Reserve pending moderated target-age review.
- **No answer confidentiality claim:** say exactly what the product controls—no typed or recorded answer, no resumable youth run—and what it does not control—a partner can hear or see and may remember what is shared.
- **Comparable peer status:** `peer` must mean no participant assesses, supervises, disciplines, mentors, or controls opportunities for the other. Ages 14 and 17 alone do not establish equal power.
- **Neutral alternative:** it must be a real activity of comparable social value, available without public explanation. Simply sitting out in view of the group is not neutral.
- **Safeguarding:** the facilitator names the institution's contact before play and follows the institution's procedure. The app must not ask follow-up questions about a real concern.
- **Institutional data:** do not collect names, pairings, answers, Pass counts, completion, screenshots, or participant-level outcomes. Ordinary preferences and hosting logs remain within the deployment's privacy assessment.

## 6. Required validation gates

### Bilingual fidelity

- The 48 implemented card pairs are otherwise close in agent, modality, intensity, time horizon, and answer scope. No additional material DE/EN card drift was found beyond the implemented changes listed.
- OFF SCRIPT Q22 formerly used `Schreibt mündlich`, which was unnatural German and more internally contradictory than EN `compose ... out loud`; the implementation removes the speech requirement in both languages.
- YOUTH WORKSHOP positioning formerly said `gleichaltrige Jugendliche` while EN said `peers aged 14 to 17`. People within that range are not necessarily the same age or on equal footing. The implemented positioning defines comparable power instead of claiming exact age equality.
- `Pass` is established product language, but German user sessions must test whether `auslassen`, `passen`, or the visible button label is clearest for 14–17-year-olds. The shipped screen and spoken introduction should use one consistent term.
- Bilingual review must be performed by people reviewing the interaction in context, not by back-translation alone. A literal match is insufficient if one version sounds more evaluative, childish, clinical, or obligatory.

### OFF SCRIPT

1. Bilingual comprehension and naturalness with Austrian German and international English users.
2. Moderated sessions covering blind/low-vision, Deaf/hard-of-hearing, speech-variable, motor-disabled, neurodivergent, and mixed-access pairs; disabled participants must be paid contributors, not only late-stage testers.
3. Quiet room, public space, seated, limited-space, and no-shared-visual-field scenarios.
4. Card-level observation of Pass, confusion, unintended contact, public attention, embarrassment, and role imbalance.
5. Reject or revise any card that only works after a facilitator invents an unrecorded accommodation.

### YOUTH WORKSHOP

1. Qualified Austrian safeguarding, education, privacy, and institutional deployment review.
2. Separate moderated sessions with ages 14–15 and 16–17; do not treat 14–17 as one validated developmental group.
3. Participants with varied disability, language, reading, communication, migration, gender, and school/youth-work contexts.
4. Test the neutral alternative and stopping path in the actual group setting, including whether passing becomes visible or stigmatizing.
5. Confirm that facilitators do not hear, collect, summarize, score, or use answers for grouping, assessment, attendance, or evidence of participation.
6. Verify the safeguarding handoff without asking participants to disclose a real concern during product research.
7. Stop the pilot for facilitator answer-harvesting, retaliation or teasing after a pass, pressured disclosure, false confidentiality beliefs, peer power mismatch, inaccessible cards without an equivalent path, or any use as assessment/intake.
