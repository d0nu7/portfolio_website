# CLOSER FR-018 to FR-020: evidence review, safety architecture, and cross-pack audit

**Review date:** 17 August 2026
**Evidence window:** database inception to 17 August 2026
**Product scope:** two adults, physically together, using one shared device; spoken answers are not entered, recorded, analysed, or stored
**Decision status:** research and editorial recommendation only; no candidate copy or catalog change in this report is approved or scientifically validated

> **Reading rule.** `Established evidence` means a finding is reasonably supported for the population and outcome actually studied. It never means that CLOSER, a proposed pack, or a proposed sequence has been validated. `Limited/indirect evidence`, `community practice`, `expert recommendation`, `editorial inference`, and `unresolved question` are deliberately kept separate throughout.

### The central evidence judgment

The strongest transferable evidence is not that a particular prompt, ritual, or touch sequence causes intimacy. It is that communication quality, voluntary self-disclosure, perceived responsiveness, and clearly available choice are associated with relational or sexual outcomes in the populations studied. A meta-analysis of 93 studies (38,499 people in current relationships) found positive associations between sexual communication and relationship satisfaction (`r = .37`) and sexual satisfaction (`r = .43`), with communication quality more strongly associated than frequency; most included evidence was cross-sectional, often one-partner self-report, so direction of causation remains unresolved ([Mallory, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000946)). This plausibly supports well-paced discussion and adjustment language, but it does **not** show that either proposed CLOSER pack will improve a relationship, consent, sexual function, or pleasure.

For FR-018, empirical BDSM literature supports heterogeneity rather than a fixed pathology, role, motivation, or identity. The best broad reviews find a growing but still predominantly descriptive and self-selected evidence base ([Brown, Barker, & Rahman, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2019.1665619); [De Neef et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esxm.2019.02.002)). Consent norms such as negotiation and safewords are real community practices, but their form varies with relationship context, and no framework makes an interaction safe by itself. A 2025 scenario experiment with 202 community-recruited BDSM practitioners found strong but context-sensitive consent norms; this is useful evidence about norms, not proof that those norms prevent harm ([Tarleton, Mackenzie, & Sagarin, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-024-03038-6)).

For FR-019, desire and arousal vary by person and context. Responsive desire and the Dual Control Model are useful ways to avoid a linear “desire → arousal → orgasm” script, but they are models, not permission to begin or continue touch ([Basson, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/00926230252851168); [Bancroft et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224490902747222)). A meta-analysis of 132 laboratory studies found only partial, heterogeneous agreement between genital and self-reported arousal; it did **not** measure willingness or consent ([Chivers et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-009-9556-9)). CLOSER must therefore neither infer nor score arousal and must never treat any physiological response as permission.

Sensate focus is a clinician-originated technique, and the literature frequently combines it with other treatments, uses clinical populations, or lacks consistent definitions. A critical review limits extrapolation to a stand-alone consumer experience ([Linschoten, Weiner, & Avery-Clark, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/14681994.2015.1127909)); a later one-page conference abstract, not a full review article, reports continuing definition and evidence gaps ([Weiner, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsxm.2022.03.350)). SLOW BURN can borrow the non-goal-oriented design principle only as an editorial inference; it must not use the therapy’s name or imply equivalent efficacy.

The review began with the five specified project documents:

Source files in `src/closer/content/` were inspected only to understand shipped versus proposed status; uncommitted product-code changes were not modified.

### 2.2 Discovery sources

Searches were conducted through:

- PubMed and PubMed Central for biomedical, sexual-health, consent, touch, pain, disability, and intervention literature;
- Crossref/DOI resolution and journal or publisher pages for bibliographic verification and sources outside PubMed;
- general web and academic-web search for discovery, backward/forward citation chasing, community-history sources, qualitative sociology, and recent online-first work; search-result snippets were never treated as evidence, and claims were checked against the underlying record/full text where accessible;
- official WHO, EU, European Commission, and ICD-11-related sources for public-health, disability, privacy, age-assurance, and classification context;
- stable community archives only where the question was the origin or contemporary meaning of a community-developed framework.

No subscription-only database search can be claimed as systematic coverage of PsycINFO, Scopus, or Web of Science. This is a structured scoping review and product evidence audit, not a registered systematic review.

The search window was database inception through **17 August 2026**. Recent online-first records were included only when a stable DOI or indexed record was available. Search results were checked for retractions or obvious corrections where discoverable; absence of a detected notice is not a guarantee.

### 2.4 Inclusion and exclusion

Included sources met at least one of these criteria:

- systematic review/meta-analysis directly addressing the construct;
- peer-reviewed human study with a relevant design and sufficiently described population;
- original validated model or instrument important to interpretation or pilot measurement;
- professional consensus or official public-health/legal source within its competence;
- community source required to document the origin or use of community practice, clearly labelled as such.

Excluded from evidentiary support were commercial wellness copy, SEO summaries, unsourced popular claims, fictional media, prompt-list blogs, pornography, and sources whose population/outcome could not support the claim being made. Clinical findings were not treated as evidence for an unsupervised consumer app. Cross-sectional associations were not worded causally. Abstract-only findings were used conservatively and identified where full text was not available.

### 2.5 Study types and recurring limitations

The review found reviews and meta-analyses, laboratory experiments, survey and scale-development studies, scenario experiments, qualitative interviews, daily-diary/experience-sampling studies, small clinical trials, and professional/community frameworks. Recurring limitations were:

- convenience, community, clinical, or university recruitment;
- predominantly Western, white, cisgender, heterosexual, monogamous, younger, educated, and able-bodied samples;
- single-partner reports rather than observed dyads;
- cross-sectional designs and common-method self-report;
- inconsistent definitions of BDSM, sex, consent, touch, desire, and satisfaction;
- intervention packages that prevent isolation of a single mechanism;
- weak coverage of disabled people, neurodivergent people, trans and intersex people, older adults, non-monogamous relationships, and non-Western contexts;
- almost no direct evidence about a two-adult, one-phone, bilingual, self-guided experience.

### 2.6 Transfer test

Every product inference was tested against four questions:

1. **Population:** Were the participants comparable to the broad adults CLOSER would invite?
2. **Mechanism:** Does the study isolate communication, touch, context, or responsiveness, or merely measure a correlated outcome?
3. **Delivery:** Was the effect observed in a clinician-led programme, laboratory, survey, existing relationship, or actual shared-device flow?
4. **Risk:** Would transferring the idea create disclosure, coercion, physical, privacy, accessibility, or legal risk not present in the study?

No reviewed study validates the proposed FR-018 bank, SLOW BURN interaction model, bilingual wording, route length, or shared-phone delivery. Those remain editorial hypotheses requiring testing.

## 3. Source-quality framework

### 3.1 Labels used in this report

| Label | Operational meaning | Permitted product language |
|---|---|---|
| **Established evidence** | Convergent review-level evidence or a strong, directly relevant design for the specific population/outcome stated. | “Research finds…” only with population, outcome, and limitation close by. |
| **Limited/indirect evidence** | Human evidence is relevant but correlational, narrow, clinical, laboratory-based, preliminary, inconsistent, or not tested in a shared-device product. | “Associated with…”, “may inform…”, “supports testing…” |
| **Community practice** | A practice developed and maintained by a community; history/use can be documented, but safety or efficacy has not been validated. | “A community-developed framework/practice…” |
| **Expert recommendation** | A conservative design or professional judgment based on risk, ethics, accessibility, clinical consensus, or law rather than demonstrated product efficacy. | “The review recommends…” |
| **Editorial inference** | A CLOSER design choice logically motivated by evidence and product constraints but not itself studied. | “Candidate”, “prototype”, “hypothesis”, “to be tested” |
| **Unresolved question** | Evidence is missing, conflicting, too weak, or outside the review’s competence. | State the gap and name the required reviewer or study. |

### 3.2 Confidence rubric

| Grade | Basis | Example in this review |
|---|---|---|
| **High for a narrow claim** | Multiple appropriate studies or a strong meta-analysis, with consistent direction; transfer may still be limited. | Communication quality is positively associated with relationship and sexual satisfaction in current-relationship samples. |
| **Moderate** | Several relevant studies or a well-supported model, but important population, causal, or delivery limits remain. | Sexual excitation and inhibition vary between and within people and with context. |
| **Low** | Small, qualitative, community-recruited, cross-sectional, clinical-package, or indirect evidence. | Specific aftercare preferences and meanings; touch benefits in an unsupervised app. |
| **Insufficient** | No suitable evidence for the product claim. | A three-act shared-phone sequence safely causes desire, trust, intimacy, or arousal. |

### 3.3 Claim discipline

The following forms are used consistently:

- **Association:** “X was associated with Y,” with no causal verb.
- **Causal finding:** reserved for random assignment or a credible longitudinal causal design, and restricted to the studied manipulation, time horizon, and sample.
- **Mechanism hypothesis:** a plausible explanation, not an observed product effect.
- **Transfer judgment:** explicitly states why evidence may or may not inform two adults using one shared phone.
- **Product recommendation:** a risk-managed editorial decision, not an empirical finding.

## 4. FR-018 findings — consensual power exchange

### 4.1 Decision and boundary

**Recommendation: conditional go for a conversation-only prototype.** The appropriate first product is structured discussion about language, fantasy, choices, negotiation, stopping, check-ins, care, and the difference between an idea and permission. It is not a BDSM tutorial, compatibility test, role-playing scene, or substitute for specialist education.

The pack must be wholly original. Do not quote, imitate, allude to, or borrow branding, characters, scenarios, signature wording, relationship dynamics, or visual language from *Fifty Shades of Grey* or another fictional/commercial property. This is an editorial and intellectual-property boundary, not a scientific finding.

This boundary follows the evidence. BDSM interests and practices are heterogeneous; broad reviews do not support treating consensual BDSM interest as inherently pathological, but the literature is still dominated by descriptive and self-selected studies ([Brown, Barker, & Rahman, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2019.1665619); [De Neef et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esxm.2019.02.002)). ICD-11’s distinction between consensual atypical interests and disorders involving distress, impairment, non-consent, or significant risk also argues against pathologising identity while preserving a clear harm boundary ([Krueger et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5487931/); [Reed et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6313247/)). Classification policy does not show that a particular practice is safe.

Additional risk and inclusion findings qualify the table:

- A U.S.-network/community survey included 2,888 kink practitioners, 767 of whom reported a consent violation; because recruitment and the analysis do not provide a population denominator for all practitioners, this documents that violations occur but is not a prevalence estimate ([Bowling et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/10778012221145299)). The transfer is a process-humility rule: neither community norms nor experienced participation makes violations impossible.
- Conceptual analyses of authority-transfer relationships identify both chosen meaning and real exit-cost concerns ([Casey & Sagarin, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-025-03240-0); [Jozkowski, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-026-03456-8)). They do not provide a validated screening tool. CLOSER should conservatively exclude dyads where work, housing, care, immigration, essential finances, reputation, or safety makes refusal costly.
- A U.S. convenience survey of 1,398 kink-involved adults reported substantial clinician nondisclosure and anticipated/perceived-stigma-related care avoidance; the self-selected, mostly white/educated sample does not support population prevalence ([Sprott et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsxm.2021.08.001)). This supports data minimisation and a hypothetical answer route, not a claim that CLOSER reduces stigma.
- A U.S. convenience sample of 398 conference/online participants found participants of colour were more likely than white participants to report racial discrimination or fetishisation ([Erickson et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-021-02102-9)). Do not generate race-play, protected-trait degradation, or stereotype prompts; transfer requires culturally varied review, not a universalised U.S. conclusion.
- A forensic literature review found only 17 published fatal partnered-BDSM cases, 15 involving strangulation/asphyxiation; case publication is highly selected and cannot estimate incidence, but the severity pattern strengthens the product-level exclusion of all neck/breath restriction ([Schori et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00414-021-02674-0)).

### 4.3 What a structured discussion could plausibly do

The plausible mechanism is **better shared information under lower social ambiguity**, not the creation of trust or consent. A well-designed pack may:

- give participants neutral language for roles, uncertainty, and “not for me”;
- separate fantasy, curiosity, discussion, prior experience, and present permission;
- normalise changes of mind and negotiated limits;
- prompt discussion of check-ins and post-interaction preferences before anyone acts;
- make external power differences discussable without eroticising them;
- offer a low-stakes way to discover that preferences differ.

These are design hypotheses derived from consent and communication research. They require moderated observation and private debriefing. They are not expected outcomes and should not appear as marketing promises.

### 4.7 Three-act editorial model

The curve increases **specificity and interpersonal relevance**, not pressure to become more explicit or to enact anything.

| Act | Working bilingual label | Editorial purpose | Appropriate content | Keep out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **NEUGIER / CURIOSITY** | Establish a non-assumptive vocabulary and permission not to know. | Meanings of power, trust, curiosity, role words, fantasy versus action, what makes a conversation feel safe. | Past trauma, technique, partner prediction, “confess your kink,” direct compatibility judgments. |
| **II** | **ABSPRACHE / NEGOTIATION** | Explore how choices could be communicated before and during a hypothetical interaction. | `yes/no/maybe/not now`, scope, check-in preferences, safewords plus ordinary stop language, changed minds, privacy, external power differences. | Enactment, bargains, persuasion, testing a limit, intoxicants, hazardous-practice instructions. |
| **III** | **FÜRSORGE / CARE** | Consider responsibility, repair, post-interaction needs, and what should remain only a conversation. | Noticing uncertainty, stopping well, response to an accidental misread, care/space/later check-in, stigma and privacy at the person’s chosen abstraction level. | Mandatory forgiveness, universal aftercare, “prove trust,” promises of future action, diagnosing coercion together. |

**Act gates:** Both people independently confirm adults-only participation before Q1. Before Act II, each independently chooses `Continue / End here`; the copy states that later questions are more direct but still conversation-only. No extra Act-III gate is needed because the pack never becomes an action flow and Pass/End remain immediate. Either `End here` produces the same neutral ending and no completion deficit. A co-present one-phone gate cannot promise anonymity because each person may infer the other’s choice; pilot the handoff for pressure and monitoring risk.

### 4.8 Routes and timing

These are **editorial estimates for pilot planning**, not evidence-derived durations:

| Route | Composition | Provisional range | Rationale and requirement |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Quick** | 12 questions, 4 per act | **18–28 min** | Complete arc without a private moment. Must not compress risk copy or use only the most explicit items. |
| **Standard** | 24 questions, 8 per act | **35–55 min** | **Default and first route to pilot**; room for pauses and follow-up. |
| **Full** | 36 questions, 12 per act | **55–85 min** | For people who knowingly choose a long discussion. Offer a visible halfway exit and never imply completion is preferable to stopping. |

No timer advances the run; no countdown is shown; ranges should be revised from observed median, interquartile range, abandon/early-end reasons, and qualitative reports. Pilot the Standard route first, then derive Quick and Full rather than testing all three simultaneously.

### 4.9 Question-design principles

1. **Ask about the participant, not the partner.** Prefer “Which words fit you today?” over “Which role is the other person?”
2. **Keep thought and permission separate in the question itself.** Add “Talking about it is not agreeing to do it” where a reasonable reader could conflate them.
3. **Permit uncertainty as content.** “I don’t know,” “it depends,” “not for me,” and “only as fantasy” must be visibly legitimate.
4. **Use conditional, bounded language.** `If you ever chose…`, `in that situation`, `for that action`, and `at that time` prevent blanket interpretations.
5. **Do not make disclosure the intimacy mechanism.** Ask for process preferences and definitions more often than secrets, histories, or lists of acts.
6. **Do not encode an escalation ladder.** Submission is not “deeper” than curiosity; pain is not more authentic than words; Act III is about responsibility, not extremity.
7. **Name power outside the fantasy.** Participation requires a real ability to decline without retaliation; erotic framing never cancels employment, financial, caregiving, immigration, housing, or reputational dependency.
8. **Use identity terms as opt-in.** Do not label participants based on answers.
9. **Keep examples category-level and low-detail.** Examples clarify a communication concept; they do not teach an activity.
10. **Make the German and English pragmatic force match.** Review `dürfen`, `sollen`, `müssen`, `möchten`, `wollen`, `consent`, `permission`, `control`, `care`, and `Grenze/limit/boundary` with native speakers and specialists.

### 4.10 Prohibited question and action patterns

- `Would you let me…?`, `How far would you go for me?`, `Do you trust me enough to…?`
- `Which of us is really dominant/submissive?`, personality diagnosis, role assignment, or gender inference.
- `Guess what your partner secretly wants`, `predict`, proxy answers, simultaneous boundary answers, or public reveal.
- A forced list of fantasies, past partners, trauma, abuse, illegal conduct, medical history, or stigmatised identity.
- “No limits,” “break a boundary,” “lose control,” “you cannot change your mind,” “earn aftercare,” or coercion framed as romance.
- Any instruction to touch, restrain, blindfold, strike, choke, gag, penetrate, use equipment/substances, or simulate punishment.
- Scores, compatibility percentages, red/green flags, countdowns, dares, forfeits, streaks, or rewards for continuing.
- A question that makes one person promise future availability or tells the other person an answer is actionable permission.
- A safeword trivia quiz that implies knowing terminology equals safe practice.

### 4.13 Moderated pilot and adverse-experience protocol

**Phase 0 — expert walkthrough.** At minimum: kink-informed sexual-health clinician/educator, trauma specialist, disability/accessibility specialist, DE and EN inclusive-language editors, safeguarding/privacy reviewer, and Austrian/EU counsel. Community expertise complements but does not replace clinical, accessibility, or legal competence.

**Phase 1 — individual cognitive interviews.** Test title, eligibility, `fantasy ≠ permission`, role terms, stop language, and each bilingual prompt with adults reading alone. Oversample newcomers, switches/role-fluid participants, LGBTQIA+ people, non-monogamous people, disabled/neurodivergent adults, older adults, and German-dominant participants. Do not require disclosure of kink identity or behaviour.

**Phase 2 — moderated dyads.** Start with approximately 24–30 diverse dyads on Standard, deliberately sampling different relationship forms and experience levels. The moderator must not hear answers; observe only interface use from outside conversational range where possible. Each participant completes a private debrief. Sample size is formative, not powered for efficacy.

Collect only research-consented, minimised measures:

- completion/early-end, Pass, act-gate decline, time per card, pause, and technical-error events;
- immediately after: custom items on pressure, autonomy, clarity, unwanted disclosure, stigma, pacing, and phone interference, clearly labelled unvalidated;
- optional pre/post **Inclusion of Other in the Self** one-item closeness measure ([Aron et al., 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.63.4.596)) and a short perceived-responsiveness measure ([Crasta et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0000986)), used descriptively rather than as proof of efficacy;
- cautiously adapted Acceptability, Appropriateness, and Feasibility items ([Weiner et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0635-3)); the measures were developed for implementation outcomes, not consent, sexual safety, or CLOSER, so DE/EN adaptation requires cognitive review and scores cannot be release thresholds;
- a private open-ended adverse-experience interview, with a later optional follow-up and a safe method to withdraw research data.

Do not collect spoken answers, fantasy content, role, body data, partner identity, or a reason for passing. Separate product telemetry consent from interview consent.

**Predefined freeze rules:** one serious event involving coercion, retaliation, unsafe joint debrief, or a consent-path bypass freezes the study and triggers independent review; two materially similar moderate adverse reports freeze the implicated card/flow; a repeated pattern of pressure or unwanted disclosure triggers rewrite or removal even if no numeric threshold is crossed. An exploratory flag such as >10–15% reporting high pressure may guide review but is not a validated safety cutoff.

### 4.14 FR-018 go/no-go conclusion

Proceed only if RaDi accepts all of these conditions: conversation-only version 1; the `POWER, BY CHOICE` boundary or an equally clear name/descriptor; independent adult eligibility and renewed opt-in; no Playful/private/saved-question mechanic; exact hazardous-content exclusions; specialist and legal review; and a staged formative pilot. **No-go** if the intended business or creative requirement is to make participants perform power exchange, prove trust, disclose secrets, or receive technique guidance inside this pack.

## 5. FR-019 findings — SLOW BURN

### 5.1 Decision and boundary

**Recommendation: conditional go for a closed, moderated prototype; no-go for public release until the physical state model, specialist reviews, and adverse-experience pilot pass.** Version 1 should be meaningfully physical—optional touch and kissing, including a receiver-named sensual area—while remaining non-penetrative, non-technique-heavy, and free of hazardous or altered-consciousness practices.

SLOW BURN is not sex therapy, sensate focus, a tantra exercise, a consent certification, or an arousal tool. It does not diagnose desire, reduce a desire discrepancy, treat pain or trauma, or lead toward a required erotic endpoint. Each card is a new invitation. `Skip` is an equal-status route choice, not failed escalation; the English negative choice should not be labelled only `Next`, because “next” can also sound like “advance.”

### 5.4 Three-act physical interaction model

The acts are **optional zones**, not levels a successful pair completes. No action is automatically queued from the previous action, and reaching Act III is not an agreement to intimate touch.

| Act | Working bilingual label | Physical scope | Example editorial flow | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **ANKOMMEN / ARRIVE** | Atmosphere, attention, no-touch options, and predictable contact in a place the receiver classifies as non-intimate for that action. | Choose position and sensory conditions; look or sit side-by-side; hands near each other; consider one exact receiver-classified place over clothing by default. | Bilateral entry; fresh exact `Yes + Yes` before every physical start or restart and every materially changed action or condition. |
| **II** | **AUFBAU / BUILD** | The receiving person shapes pace, pressure, movement, area, and closeness; repetition and return are valid. “Build” means richer information or responsiveness, not necessarily more intensity. | Receiver chooses area and demonstrates/asks for pace or pressure; giver follows; swap only after a fresh gate; embrace, sensual-external touch, and kissing each have their own gates. | Renewed act opt-in does not authorise touch; every physical start/restart and material change still needs a fresh exact `Yes + Yes`. |
| **III** | **WÄHLEN / CHOOSE** | Public V1: any earlier category, optional longer kissing, and eligible sensual external touch. **Consumer version-1 boundary:** categories 0–6 only—presence through receiver-named sensual external touch and kissing, without intimate-area or exposed-intimate technique. C06/category 7 is the sole beyond-V1 candidate: it may appear only in a separately approved later moderated-prototype cohort, over clothing, with no app-suggested anatomy, penetration, or technique. It is excluded from public V1 regardless of early pilot acceptability.

| Tier | Category | Default | Required choices | Version-1 status |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Presence/no touch | Environment, distance, position, device placement; no forced eye contact or synchronised breathing | Shared setting and entry choices | **Core candidate** |

Moving between tiers is never automatic or one-way. A pair may remain at Tier 0, alternate tiers, choose one action, or end. `Skip` moves to an equal-status alternative or close; it does not silently select a “safer” touch. A request for **less pressure, slower movement, more space, no touch, Pause, Stop, or End** acts immediately. A different place, action, role, movement, combination, duration, or any increase must stop, be named exactly, and obtain a new Yes + Yes—even when the proposed destination is editorially “lower.” Body areas are not inherently neutral or intimate: if a participant classifies a proposed area as more intimate than the routing label does, the participant’s classification controls.

### 5.6 Minimal bilateral choice and check-in system

#### Before starting

1. **Eligibility/setting screen:** both adults; voluntarily chose a touch experience; sober and able to choose clearly; a lawful place with enough privacy; either person can move away or leave; no driving, caregiving, work duty, active conflict, feared consequence, or urgent pain/medical concern. This is not a diagnostic or legal-capacity screen and does not certify a setting as safe.
2. **Per-category eligibility set:** one at a time, each person marks every category they are willing to *consider* in this session. The set is non-contiguous: for example, kissing can remain eligible while scalp touch does not. Routing sees only the intersection of the two sets. The choices are RAM-only and destroyed on exit.
3. **Signal plan:** both review the ordinary spoken signals and may add one accessible deliberate signal if speech is not usable. No blindfold, gag, headphones, or other condition may remove access to that signal.

Preselection is only a content filter. It is **not** consent to a later card.

#### Before every physical start or restart, and every materially changed action or condition

Each person handles the same phone in turn while the other looks away and chooses:

| Choice | Meaning | System behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **YES / JA** | “I choose this exact invitation now.” | Continue only if both choose Yes. |
| **ADJUST / ANPASSEN** | “A changed version might work.” | Show a neutral adjustment menu: `different area / over clothing / lighter or firmer / slower or faster / still or moving / shorter / more space or a different position / role / no touch / skip / end`, then repeat the bilateral gate. |
| **SKIP / AUSLASSEN** | “Do not do this; move on without discussion.” | Show `We’ll skip this one / Das lassen wir aus`; never say who selected it; move to an equal-status alternative or offer End. |

This is **not a private or anonymous ballot**: a participant knows their own choice and may infer the other’s. It therefore cannot make a coercive dyad safe. Alternate who chooses first; mask the first choice and all accessible-state cues; reveal only the least-permissive combined result; then clear both raw choices. No sound, colour flash, celebration, wait animation, or completion score should make `Yes` more attractive than `Adjust` or `Skip`. If the product must retain the word `Next`, pair it with explicit `Skip this / Überspringen`; never use bare `Next` as the negative choice.

| Participant A | Participant B | Shared result / system rule |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | Yes | Open only the displayed category/action under the displayed conditions; either person may still change or stop. |
| Yes | Adjust | Show the neutral adjustment menu; do not identify who chose Adjust; re-gate the revised proposition. |
| Adjust | Adjust | Same neutral adjustment flow; no persuasion screen. |
| Any | Skip | Close the proposition and move to an equal-status alternative or close; ask for no reason. |
| Any | Pause | Stop all interaction and show only pause/de-escalation/end choices. |
| Any | End | Clear volatile state and show the neutral end. |
| No response | Any | Unresolved and closed to action; no timeout, inferred yes, or countdown. |
| Background, lock, refresh, crash, or visibility loss | Any | End the session and clear volatile state; never resume at a gate or action. |

#### During touch

The app introduces these plain-language controls before the first action and keeps them available without requiring the phone:

| Deutsch | English | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Mehr.** | **More.** | Stop first; name the exact proposed increase and obtain a new Yes + Yes before resuming. |
| **Sanfter.** | **Softer.** | Reduce pressure or intensity immediately. |
| **Langsamer.** | **Slower.** | Reduce pace immediately; it is not an invitation to continue indefinitely. |
| **Nicht dort.** | **Not there.** | Leave that area immediately; do not move to another area without an exact new proposal and Yes + Yes. |
| **Anders.** | **Different.** | Stop the action. Name the exact change and re-gate it; the word itself authorises nothing. |
| **Pause.** | **Pause.** | Stop all actions; resuming even an already-open exact action requires a new Yes + Yes. Offer more space and End equally. |
| **Stopp.** | **Stop.** | End the current touch immediately; no confirmation or explanation. |

`End / Beenden` remains a persistent interface control that closes the whole session and destroys transient state; it is not merely another touch-adjustment word. Participants may use any other word, sound, deliberate signal, or communication aid that works for them. Any sign of uncertainty is a reason to stop and ask, not evidence of consent. The interface must never claim to interpret non-verbal signals. The giver does not argue with, rephrase, or repeatedly re-offer a declined action.

### 5.7 Pacing, timing, screen use, and handoffs

- **Event-driven checks:** gate every new physical invitation/restart and every material change in category, area, clothing, movement, duration, combination, role, exposure, or intensity. Do not interrupt an unchanged continuing action at fixed intervals and do not rely on a periodic check to cure ambiguity.
- **No automatic progress:** no countdown, timed “hold,” streak, autoplay, background vibration, or completion pressure. The next card appears only when either person picks up the phone and both are ready to choose.
- **One concise card:** preview → bilateral choice → instruction. Avoid scrolling during a physical action.
- **Phone secondary:** after reading, put it face down on a stable surface within either person’s reach. Do not make one participant hold it while touching or repeatedly pass it mid-action.
- **Screen return:** picking up/waking the phone shows equally weighted `Same / Adjust / Skip / Pause / End`; `Same` proposes the exact prior action and still requires a fresh masked Yes + Yes before any contact resumes.
- **No lock-screen disclosure:** notifications and previews are suppressed where the platform permits. The app never puts the action/category in a system notification.
- **Prototype routes:** use fewer screen interruptions than the conversation packs: Quick ≈15–25 minutes with 9 invitations (3/3/3), Standard ≈30–50 with 15 (5/5/5; first moderated prototype), and **Unhurried** ≈45–70 with 21 (7/7/7; only after burden testing). These are planning ranges, never displayed targets. Avoid `Full`, which may imply maximum intensity. Gates are not “rounds”; repetition happens away from the phone; actual ranges must come from pilot distributions.

### 5.9 Inclusive language and accessibility requirements

**Language**

- Use `person giving touch` / `berührende Person` and `person receiving touch` / `empfangende Person`, or “one of you / the other person.” Avoid `man/woman`, `masculine/feminine`, active/passive, and anatomy-based role assumptions.
- Say `an area you name`, not a presumed breast, chest, vulva, penis, vagina, anus, limb, skin type, or erogenous zone. When an anatomical term is medically or legally necessary, have DE/EN specialists review exact wording and trans/intersex inclusion.
- `Pleasant`, `comfortable`, `wanted`, `interesting`, `sensual`, and `arousing` are not synonyms. Ask only about the property needed for the choice.
- Never equate movement, stillness, erection, lubrication, orgasm, laughter, freezing, eye contact, or silence with wanting.
- Avoid “normal body,” “both hands,” “stand,” “look into their eyes,” “relax,” “let go,” and “just breathe.” Offer concrete alternatives.

**Mobility and physical access**

- Every card must work seated, reclined, side-by-side, or in another stable position chosen by the pair. Never require kneeling, standing, weight-bearing, twisting, reaching behind the body, floor transfer, or equal range of motion.
- A participant may guide with words, demonstration, an assistive device, or an agreed deliberate signal. Do not touch mobility aids, prostheses, medical devices, scars, painful areas, hair, or sensory equipment unless the person specifically names them.
- Either person may keep clothing on and may change position or support at any time. Position change never counts as consent to continue.
- Provide a no-touch route through Act I and an end that does not require a hug, kiss, eye contact, or spoken appreciation.

**Sensory and cognitive access**

- Preview light, sound, music, scent, fabric, temperature, touch area, pace, pressure, movement, and duration as adjustable—not as a “romantic” default.
- Use literal one-action sentences, persistent icon-plus-text controls, adequate contrast, screen-reader labels, zoom/reflow, reduced motion, and no colour-only meaning. Traffic-light colours may be offered only with words.
- Do not require rapid decisions, memory of previous consent, or interpreting metaphor. `Adjust` always displays the available words again.
- Provide advance content/category preview so surprise is not the entertainment mechanism.

These rules reduce obvious barriers; they do not make one design universally accessible. Formative testing must include disabled, chronically ill, neurodivergent, Deaf/hard-of-hearing, blind/low-vision, speech-disabled, and older adults, paid for their expertise.

### 5.11 Entry, adjustment, de-escalation, and ending

| Moment | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Entry** | Neutral 18+ preview; independent co-present choices; setting/capacity exclusion; non-contiguous per-category eligibility sets; ordinary stop language; explicit statement that eligibility is not permission for a card. |
| **Adjustment** | Stop the current action first. Less pressure, slower movement, more space, no touch, Stop, and End take effect immediately. Any different place/action/role/movement/combination/duration or increase is named exactly and begins only after a new Yes + Yes. |

### 5.14 Moderated pilot and adverse-experience protocol

**Phase 0 — independent safety review.** Sexual-health clinician/educator; trauma/safeguarding specialist; disability and inclusive-design specialists; DE/EN editors; privacy/security engineer; Austrian/EU counsel. Review every action, state transition, recovery path, research procedure, and support route.

**Phase 1 — individual and non-touch cognitive walkthroughs.** Approximately 12–16 dyads’ worth of participants first review alone, then in pairs without enacting touch. Test whether they understand the non-contiguous eligibility sets and their intersection, the just-in-time combined ballot, `Adjust`, inference limits, spoken Stop, background expiry, and the non-goal framing. Include varied bodies, mobility, sensory profiles, communication methods, orientations, genders, relationship forms, experience, and DE/EN dominance.

**Phase 2 — restricted moderated enactment.** Approximately 30–40 dyads in small cohorts, beginning with categories 0–2; add categories 3–4 only after cohort review, then test sensual external touch (5) and kissing (6) in separately reviewed steps. Category 7/C06, if approved at all, is isolated in a later cohort and remains over clothing. A moderator never sees/hears choices or touch details and can stop the research protocol without mediating the relationship. Participants have a private physical space, immediate exit, separate debrief access, and no payment structure that rewards completion.

**Outcomes—do not measure arousal or orgasm:**

- comprehension of each state and correct response to scenario probes;
- action/category shown, combined skip/adjust, Pause/End, time, abandonment, and technical errors in a dedicated consented research build—never individual consent choices or body areas;
- private custom ratings of autonomy, pressure, comfort, pleasantness, clarity, body self-consciousness, sensory fit, pain change, accessibility, partner responsiveness, and phone interference; label them unvalidated;
- cautiously adapted Acceptability, Appropriateness, and Feasibility measures ([Weiner et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0635-3)); they are implementation outcomes, not validated measures of consent, sexual safety, or CLOSER, and require DE/EN cognitive review; optional descriptive IOS/responsiveness measures likewise do not test efficacy;
- open-ended private debrief and opt-in 24–48-hour follow-up for delayed discomfort, conflict, pain, shame, or unwanted disclosure.

**Adverse-event classes:**

- **Serious:** stop ignored; sexual boundary violation; coercion/retaliation; injury; loss of capacity; panic/dissociation requiring support; need for medical, safeguarding, or crisis help; sensitive-data exposure.
- **Moderate:** unwanted touch; freeze/appeasement response; repeated pressure; meaningful pain flare; sensory overload; body-shame distress; category ballot misunderstood; inaccessible Stop/End.
- **Mild/product friction:** awkward wording, small unwanted interruption corrected immediately, phone distraction, unclear card, or positioning inconvenience without ongoing impact.

One serious event freezes the entire implicated tier and protocol. Two materially similar moderate events freeze the action/transition. Any consent-state bypass, persistence of sensitive state, or crash that resumes an action is a release blocker. Numeric signals such as >10–15% high-pressure or unwanted-touch reports are conservative exploratory review triggers, not validated safety thresholds. Resume only after independent review, correction, re-consent, and regression testing.

### 5.15 FR-019 go/no-go conclusion

**Go** for the staged prototype only if RaDi accepts: a physical but non-penetrative version-1 ceiling; independent category filtering plus action-level bilateral choice; ordinary spoken stop always sufficient; no inference from bodily response; no sensitive persistence/resume; full accessibility and adverse-event work; and specialist/legal approval. **No-go** for public release until the restricted pilot shows high comprehension, no serious events, no repeated moderate pattern, and acceptable accessibility/pressure outcomes. **No-go entirely** if the desired design requires automatic escalation, surprise, intoxication, restraint, hazardous practice, genital technique, arousal scoring, tantra/therapy claims, or a guaranteed erotic endpoint.

### 6.5 Translation and accessibility findings

Material DE/EN mismatches are: First Date Q25/Q34 (`soll` versus `hope`), Date Night Q25 (`sich fallen lassen` versus `let your guard down`), Friends Q24 (neutral `Thema` versus problem-loaded `dealing with`), Deep Q34 (`Ruhe` versus `space`), and Late Night Q22 (`Lust` versus `pleasure`). Natural equivalence—not literal word identity—is the target, but independent DE/EN cognitive interviews must test agency, obligation, intimacy, valence, modality, and time horizon. Translation/adaptation guidance supports cultural review and empirical checking, but CLOSER questions are not psychometric instruments ([International Test Commission](https://www.intestcom.org/files/guideline_test_adaptation_2ed.pdf); [U.S. Census cognitive-interviewing method](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2008/adrm/ssm2008-02.html)).

Cross-pack access requirements:

- Revise the product rule from “answers must be spoken” to **“answers stay outside CLOSER”** if RaDi accepts signing, AAC, participant-controlled typing on a separate tool, drawing, or another mutually understood method. Never let a partner answer by proxy unless the person explicitly directs facilitation. AAC evidence supports adapting to a person’s communication method in the studied support settings, not validation of CLOSER ([Hanley et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/17446295221115914)).
- `NO THINKING`, countdown, simultaneous speech, eye-contact, whisper, and speed mechanics need equivalent slower/nonvisual/nonauditory paths. W3C cognitive-accessibility guidance supports enough time and adaptable presentation, not any pack outcome ([W3C Cognitive Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/cognitive/)).
- Do not score memory detail, agreement, elaboration, or speed. In two hypothetical-scenario studies (total `N = 714` European American and Asian participants), perceived closeness differed by information/memory type and cultural group; this was not an observed CLOSER conversation and cannot define a universal “good answer” ([Guan & Wang, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/00220221211072809)).
- Keep relationship plurality explicit: no assumed monogamy, marriage, cohabitation, sex, renewed friendship, biological family, shared childhood, parenthood, home ownership, travel privilege, alcohol, or future contact.
- A manager, assessor, teacher, mentor, clinician, caregiver, client, scholarship decision-maker, or event organiser may make participation non-voluntary regardless of friendly copy. Exclude leveraged pairings rather than “screening” them through questions.

### 6.7 FR-020 validation and release gate

Run base and eligible Playful sessions separately. Ask each participant privately about relevance, clarity, pressure, experienced intensity, phone intrusion, feeling heard, recovery, and route choice; do not code answer content. Automatic review triggers are repeated paraphrase mismatch; two or more participants in a small cell reading a card as promise/permission/test; concentrated Pass/End at one ID; route time repeatedly outside its range; visible distress without easy recovery; inaccessible mechanics; or a specialist-identified foreseeable harm. One credible coercion, workplace-retaliation, road-safety, sexual-consent, or privacy failure pauses that feature path; it is never averaged away.

Release requires resolved P0 items, independent bilingual sign-off, named specialist sign-off, physical-device/accessibility checks, operational-only analytics, and an explicit RaDi decision for each content proposal. Appendix A contains the atomic proposals; their presence is not approval.

### 7.1 Distinct jobs for the three adult experiences

| Dimension | LATE NIGHT | POWER, BY CHOICE (FR-018) | SLOW BURN (FR-019) |
|---|---|---|---|
| User intent | Have an explicit adult conversation | Discuss negotiated power/kink language and choices | Choose a guided physical experience |

| Permission boundary | No answer authorises action | Fantasy, role, trust, and discussion never authorise action | Only the current bilateral action choice authorises the current invitation; it can change immediately |
| Intensity curve | More explicit topics | More specific responsibility and negotiation | Higher optional physical category; never automatic |
| Physical action | None | None in version 1 | Yes, within the version-1 ceiling |

| Private moment | Readiness only; no secret content | None | Bilateral safety choices only; no entertainment task |

**Overlap rule:** a prompt belongs in LATE NIGHT if its purpose is desire/sexual-life discussion without a power-negotiation construct. It belongs in POWER, BY CHOICE if power, role, authority, negotiation, stopping, or post-scene responsibility is necessary to the question. It belongs in SLOW BURN only if the interaction itself is an action-level touch choice. Duplicated prompts should not be used to funnel users from talk into action.

### 7.5 Unsupported claims to prohibit

- “Scientifically proven,” “clinically validated,” “therapist-approved,” or “evidence-based intimacy” for any CLOSER-authored sequence.
- “The 36 questions make people fall in love.” The Aron et al. procedure studied experimentally generated interpersonal closeness under specific conditions, not guaranteed love or universal outcomes ([Aron et al., 1997](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003)).
- Any claim that CLOSER creates trust, ensures consent, detects coercion, prevents assault, repairs relationships, treats trauma, fixes desire discrepancy, improves sexual function, causes arousal/orgasm, or guarantees pleasure.
- BDSM is caused by trauma/pathology; BDSM practitioners are psychologically healthier; dominant people lack/have more empathy; submissive people are passive; role is gender, personality, or destiny.
- SSC, RACK, PRICK, the 4Cs, safewords, traffic lights, check-ins, or an app gate make an action safe or legally consensual.
- Aftercare is always required, always physical, prevents a universal “drop,” or proves that a scene was ethical.
- Responsive desire means someone should allow touch until desire appears.

## 8. Product recommendations

### 8.1 Decisions to encode before content implementation

1. **Separate engines/contracts.** Conversation packs compile fixed questions; SLOW BURN compiles action states with transient bilateral choices. Reuse visual primitives, not consent semantics.
2. **Pack-specific gates.** A shared collapsed `18+` library disclosure is only visibility. Each adult pack supplies eligibility, participation, escalation, decline, expiry, and support rules.
3. **Monotonic safety.** Conversation Pass and physical-action Skip/Pause/End always dominate progress. De-escalation is immediate; escalation is impossible without two new current choices.
4. **No adult-action resume.** Persisting a question pack may be acceptable under the existing contract; persisting or restoring a touch action/consent state is not.
5. **No sensitive telemetry by default.** Threat-model URLs, logs, analytics, error reports, browser history, screenshots/app switcher, notifications, storage, backups, and support tickets.
6. **Exact bilingual content objects.** German and English are reviewed together; no runtime machine translation and no silent fallback from missing copy.

## 9. Research gaps

1. **No direct product evidence.** No located study tests a bilingual, one-phone, two-adult CLOSER flow for negotiated-power conversation or action-level touch.
2. **Causal communication evidence is thin.** Meta-analytic associations are useful but cannot show that adding prompts improves satisfaction, consent quality, or relationship outcomes.
3. **BDSM research coverage is uneven.** Community convenience samples overrepresent people who are visible, connected, Western, white, educated, and willing to participate. Newcomers, private practitioners, disabled people, older adults, and non-Western contexts need better representation.
4. **Consent measures are not app safety validators.** Internal/external consent scales describe experiences; they do not certify an action flow, diagnose coercion, or settle law.
5. **Safeword/check-in harm outcomes are largely untested.** Norm adoption and perceived acceptability are not observed prevention effectiveness.
6. **Aftercare evidence is mostly qualitative/community-based.** Comparative effects, unwanted aftercare, disability/access needs, and delayed outcomes remain under-studied.
7. **Sensate-focus transfer is unknown.** Existing literature often involves clinicians, treatment-seeking populations, multi-week practice, and combined interventions.
8. **Touch category ordering is editorial.** There is no universal empirical ladder from hand contact to kissing to intimate touch; individual and cultural ordering can reverse.
9. **Disability/neurodivergence evidence is insufficient.** Small qualitative samples cannot specify safe defaults; co-design and access-mode testing are essential.
10. **Trauma outcomes are unknown.** Choice-led design may reduce obvious risks but cannot promise non-triggering or therapeutic benefit.
11. **Shared-phone effects are unknown.** Existing phone-presence/phubbing work is indirect and may not generalise to a deliberately shared facilitator.
12. **DE/EN pragmatic equivalence needs empirical review.** Consent, desire, power, care, family, workplace, and humour wording can differ in force beyond literal translation.
13. **Duration and recovery are unvalidated.** Sensitive packs may produce longer answers, pauses, fatigue, or delayed emotional load not captured by existing estimates.
14. **Age assurance and regulation are moving targets.** Recheck immediately before release with counsel and platform policies.
15. **FR-020 prioritisation needs user-session evidence.** Text review can identify plausible risks, but real dyads are needed to distinguish a theoretical concern from experienced pressure or confusion.

Nachdem die empfangende Person die genaue Stelle benannt hat und der Bildschirm „ein kurzer Kontakt“ anzeigt, wählen beide erneut; nur Ja + Ja öffnet die Option. Beide Personen können vor oder während des Kontakts stoppen.

##### C07 — Longer kissing, self-paced

**EN**
For an already-open mouth kiss, consider continuing without a timer. Obtain a new Yes + Yes before continuing. Either person can create distance at any moment; distance means stop and check, not follow.

**DE**
Zieht bei einem bereits geöffneten Kuss auf den Mund in Betracht, ohne Zeitvorgabe weiterzumachen. Holt vor dem Fortsetzen ein neues Ja + Ja ein. Beide Personen können jederzeit Abstand schaffen; Abstand bedeutet stoppen und nachfragen, nicht folgen.

##### C08 — One opened touch with kissing

**EN**
Consider combining one already-open kiss with one already-open external touch. Name both before the gate. If Yes + Yes opens the combination, begin one action first; add the second only after a second masked bilateral choice also resolves to Yes + Yes.

**DE**
Zieht in Betracht, einen bereits geöffneten Kuss mit einer bereits geöffneten äußerlichen Berührung zu verbinden. Benennt beides vor der Entscheidung. Wenn Ja + Ja die Kombination öffnet, beginnt mit einer Handlung; fügt die zweite erst hinzu, nachdem auch eine zweite verdeckte beidseitige Entscheidung zu Ja + Ja geführt hat.

##### C09 — Change one detail, not intensity

**EN**
Choose an already-open action. A request for less, slower, more space, or Stop takes effect immediately. For more, faster, movement, longer duration, or another place—even inside an open boundary—name the revised action and obtain Yes + Yes before resuming.

**DE**
Wählt eine bereits offene Handlung. Eine Bitte um weniger, langsamer, mehr Abstand oder Stopp gilt sofort. Für mehr, schneller, Bewegung, längere Dauer oder eine andere Stelle – auch innerhalb einer geöffneten Grenze – benennt die geänderte Handlung und holt vor dem Fortsetzen Ja + Ja ein.

##### C10 — Optional role switch

**EN**
Would switching roles for one already-open action fit now? Name the exact switched action and choose separately; only Yes + Yes opens it. One person's wish for reciprocity never creates an obligation for the other.

**DE**
Würde ein Rollenwechsel für eine bereits offene Handlung jetzt passen? Benennt die genaue Handlung mit getauschten Rollen und wählt getrennt; nur Ja + Ja öffnet sie. Der Wunsch einer Person nach Gegenseitigkeit verpflichtet die andere Person nicht.

##### C11 — No-touch reset

**EN**
Choose a no-touch pause. Each person finds a comfortable position and distance. Nothing needs to happen next. If an exact open action is proposed again, it resumes only after a new Yes + Yes; Close and End remain equal choices.

**DE**
Wählt eine Pause ohne Berührung. Beide Personen finden eine bequeme Position und einen passenden Abstand. Danach muss nichts passieren. Wenn eine genaue offene Handlung erneut vorgeschlagen wird, beginnt sie erst nach einem neuen Ja + Ja; Abschließen und Beenden bleiben gleichwertige Optionen.

##### C12 — Choose the close

**EN**
Move to a close without adding a new touch category. Choose separately: space · quiet nearness · an already-open handhold · an already-open embrace · water or practical comfort · one check-in sentence · end now.

**DE**

#### C.6 Finale candidates — CARE AND SPACE / FÜRSORGE UND RAUM

##### F01 — Space

**EN**
Choose the amount of space that fits now. No touch and leaving the room are valid. You do not need to discuss the session before closing.

**DE**
Wählt den Abstand, der jetzt passt. Keine Berührung und den Raum zu verlassen sind gültige Optionen. Ihr müsst die Session vor dem Schließen nicht besprechen.

##### F02 — Quiet nearness

**EN**
If both choose it, stay near each other without adding touch. Look wherever is comfortable. End whenever either person wants.

**DE**
Wenn beide es wählen, bleibt ohne zusätzliche Berührung beieinander. Schaut dorthin, wo es angenehm ist. Beendet, sobald eine Person möchte.

##### F03 — Already-open touch

**EN**
If both choose it, use one already-open handhold or embrace. Do not introduce a new place or stronger version. Either person ends first.

**DE**
Wenn beide es wählen, nutzt ein bereits geöffnetes Händehalten oder eine bereits geöffnete Umarmung. Führt keine neue Stelle und keine stärkere Variante ein. Beide Personen können zuerst beenden.

##### F04 — One check-in sentence

**EN**
Optional: each may complete one sentence—“Right now, I would like ___.” Passing is equal. Listen without fixing, defending, or asking for details.

**DE**
Optional: Beide können einen Satz vervollständigen – „Im Moment wünsche ich mir ___.“ Auslassen ist gleichwertig. Hört zu, ohne etwas lösen oder verteidigen zu müssen und ohne nach Details zu fragen.

##### F05 — Neutral end

**EN**
This session is closed. Nothing was saved. What happens next is a new choice; the session does not authorize any further action.

**DE**
Diese Session ist beendet. Es wurde nichts gespeichert. Was danach passiert, ist eine neue Entscheidung; die Session erlaubt keine weitere Handlung.

#### C.7 Content routing notes

- Quick should use A01/A02 plus one opened touch candidate, one recipient-direction candidate, a direction gate, one Act II action inside the shared eligible set, C01, and a close. It must not use C06.
- Standard should deliberately alternate physical and choice/rest cards; do not place two new physical-action invitations back-to-back without a choice/rest card.
- Unhurried should add repetition and no-touch space, not merely more intense candidates.
- A route compiler must validate prerequisite categories. For example, B12 requires an opened kiss; C03 requires one exact opened external area; C08 requires both components separately opened.
- Rejected, adjusted-away, or closed categories are removed from automatic routing. No random fallback can select them.
- Card order should be curated and reviewed in DE and EN. Random shuffle is unsafe because prerequisites and recovery spacing matter.
